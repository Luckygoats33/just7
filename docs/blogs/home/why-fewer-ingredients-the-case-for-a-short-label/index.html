Why Fewer Ingredients? The Case for a Short Label

In this article
  1. Every ingredient has a job
  2. What we left out
  3. Why short beats long
  4. The lineup

We started with one question: what does a laundry sheet actually need? Then we kept asking until the list stopped getting shorter.

Quick answer: fewer ingredients make a label easier to read, easier to check and easier to trust. Every ingredient in Just 7 has a job. Nothing is there for show.

Every ingredient has a job

IngredientIts job
Plant glycerinKeeps the sheet flexible
WaterHelps make the sheet
Decyl glucosideLifts dirt
Lauryl glucosideLifts oily dirt
Sodium citrateSoftens water
Sodium carbonateBoosts cleaning
Pullulan filmHolds it all together

Seven ingredients. Seven jobs. Nobody's sitting around on the clock.

What we left out

  • Fragrance, so your laundry smells like clean fabric.
  • Dyes, because a sheet doesn't need a color.
  • PVA film, because we chose a pullulan film instead (read it).
  • Sulfates, because glucosides do the job for us.
  • Essential oils, for the sake of pets (read it).

Why short beats long

  1. Easy to read. You can check it in one breath.
  2. Easy to compare. Count, then read (read it).
  3. Easy to trust. Nothing hides behind a vague word.

The lineup

  • Our gentle sheet: the shortest list we could make.
  • Just 7: our everyday hero.
  • Just 9: adds enzymes and a chelator for tough jobs.

Article 49 helps you choose.

FAQ

Do fewer ingredients clean as well? A well-designed short formula can clean well. Every ingredient just has to earn its place.†

What are the 7 ingredients in Just 7? Plant glycerin, water, decyl glucoside, lauryl glucoside, sodium citrate, sodium carbonate and a pullulan film made from fermented starch.

Why not fewer? Our gentle sheet has five. Just 7 adds two for extra cleaning power.

Are long ingredient lists bad? Not necessarily. They're just harder to read and compare.
